## 1. Don't Touch Leverage First

The first thing many new traders do when they open the Futures page:

- Check how many× they can use
- 5×, 10×, 20×, 50×

The better order is:

**Judge the trade first → then decide leverage.**

Leverage is just a position tool. It is NOT a trading reason.

---

## 2. Step 1: Is There Even a Trade Opportunity?

Before opening anything, ask:

- Is the market trending or ranging?
- Is price at a high or low area?
- Did it just break out?
- Is it pulling back?
- Is there key support/resistance ahead?
- Did I miss the entry already?

If you can't answer these clearly, the best move may be:

**Don't open anything yet.**

---

## 3. Look at the Bigger Timeframe First, Then the Small One

A common mistake: only watching the 1m or 5m chart.

Example:
- 5m suddenly pumps
- You instantly Long

But if the 1H and 4H are still in a clear downtrend, that pump might just be a bounce.

Simple rule:

**Big timeframe = direction**
**Small timeframe = entry location**

---

## 4. Step 2: Know Exactly What Logic You're Trading

Before entry, say it in one sentence:

"Why am I entering right now?"

Examples:

**Long logic:**
Breakout, then successful retest of support.

**Short logic:**
Rejection at resistance, momentum turns weak.

**Trend logic:**
Higher High + Higher Low continuation.

If you can't say it in one sentence, the trade probably isn't clear yet.

---

## 5. Don't Open Just Because a Candle Got Big

See a huge green candle → instantly Long.
See a huge red candle → instantly Short.

But a big candle can mean:

- Trend just started
- Move is already late
- Short squeeze
- Long liquidation cascade
- News-driven volatility

So:
**Big candle = bigger volatility. Not automatically a good entry.**

---

## 6. Step 3: Find the Invalidation Before the Entry

### Invalidation = the condition that proves your idea wrong

Example:
You want to Long because price is holding a support level.

If that support breaks clearly, your Long thesis is invalid.

So before entry, know:

**Where am I wrong → then exit.**

That matters more than guessing where price "must" go.

---

## 7. Why Stop Comes Before Position Size

Assume:

- Entry: 70,000
- Reasonable stop: 68,500
- That's a wide stop

If you still open a huge position, one stop-hit can exceed the loss you were willing to take.

Correct order:

1. Define stop distance
2. Then calculate position size

---

## 8. Step 4: Set Your Max Risk Per Trade

Example account: 1,000 USDT

Decide in advance:

- 10 USDT max loss?
- 20 USDT?
- 30 USDT?

There's no "perfect number." The point is:

**You know your max risk before you enter.**

Don't wait until you're already losing to ask:
"Should I cut or not?"

---

## 9. Position Size Should Change With Stop Distance

Two trades:

**Trade A:** stop distance 1%
**Trade B:** stop distance 3%

If both use the same size, Trade B carries 3× the risk.

Rule:

**Wider stop → smaller position.**
That's real risk control.

---

## 10. Step 5: Decide Leverage LAST

Only after:

- Entry is clear
- Stop is clear
- Position size is clear

…then choose leverage.

Leverage becomes an execution tool — not an impulse amplifier.

---

## 11. 10× Isn't Automatically More Dangerous Than 5×

Example:

**Trader A:** 10× but tiny position
**Trader B:** 5× but almost all-in

Trader B may be taking more real risk.

So don't judge a trade by leverage alone.

---

## 12. Isolated vs Cross Margin

### Isolated
Risk is more contained to that position's margin. Easier for beginners to understand "worst-case loss."

### Cross
More account balance can support the position, but one bad trade can affect the whole account.

For beginners, the key isn't "which is best."
It's: do you understand what happens to your funds in extreme moves?

---

## 13. Step 6: Limit or Market?

If you already have a clear entry zone → use **Limit Order**.

Example:
- BTC now: 70,500
- You only want Long near 70,000
- Set a limit and wait

---

## 14. When Is Market Order Reasonable?

Use Market when:

- Breakout continues fast
- Your strategy needs priority fill

But remember:

**More volatility = higher slippage risk.**

Market = convenient, but less control over fill price.

---

## 15. Step 7: Confirm Stop-Loss Immediately After Fill

After the order fills, don't first check:

"How much profit do I have?"

First confirm:

**Is my stop actually set?**

In fast moves, you may not have time to fix it manually.

---

## 16. Don't Widen the Stop Just Because Price Is Getting Close

Example:

- Original stop: 68,500
- Price drops to 68,600
- You think: "give it more room"
- Change to 68,000
- Then 67,500

This turns a defined risk into an expanding risk.

---

## 17. Ask: Is the Trade Logic Still Valid?

When price approaches your stop, don't ask:

"Can I wait a little longer?"

Ask:

"Is the original structure still intact?"

If the invalidation condition already triggered, the reason you entered is gone.

---

## 18. Step 8: Don't Close Profitable Trades Too Early

Many beginners:

- Hold losers too long
- Close winners at +0.5% or +1%

Result:

Small wins + big losses.

Instead, follow your pre-planned take-profit.

---

## 19. Use Partial Take-Profit

Example:

- Entry: 70,000
- Target 1: 72,000
- Target 2: 73,500

At Target 1, reduce position.
Let the rest ride.

This locks some profit while keeping trend exposure.

---

## 20. After Target 1, You Don't Have to Close Everything

If structure is still strong:

- Close part
- Move stop up
- Let remainder run

More flexible than "close all at first profit."

---

## 21. When Does Break-Even Make Sense?

Not at +0.2%.

Better when:

- Price has moved a meaningful distance
- New support formed
- Structure moved in your favor

Then consider break-even.

---

## 22. Too-Early Break-Even Can Get Stopped by Normal Pullback

Example:

- Entry: 70,000
- Price only reaches 70,300
- You move stop to 70,000
- Normal pullback to 69,950 stops you out
- Then price continues up

Break-even ≠ earlier is better.

---

## 23. Step 9: Know When NOT to Add

Most dangerous add reason:

"I'm losing, so I'll average down."

If support already broke and you keep adding, you're just scaling up a wrong position.

---

## 24. A Valid Add Needs Confirmation

Example:

After first Long:

- New breakout high
- Pullback holds
- Higher Low forms

Now adding is based on **strengthening structure**, not "price is cheaper."

---

## 25. After Adding, Recalculate Total Risk

After adding:

- Average entry changes
- Total position changes
- Total risk changes

Don't just look at the new piece.
Look at the whole position's worst-case loss.

---

## 26. Step 10: Don't Wait for Liquidation to Admit You're Wrong

Liquidation is NOT a normal exit.

If a trade only ends by liquidation, something went wrong earlier:

- Position size
- Stop-loss
- Leverage
- Risk control

---

## 27. Stop-Loss ≠ Liquidation

**Stop-loss:** you exit on your own terms.
**Liquidation:** the system force-closes you because margin is too low.

Goal: end wrong trades before liquidation.

---

## 28. Funding Rate Also Affects Holding Cost

With Perpetual Futures, holding long-term means watching **Funding Rate**.

Contract cost isn't just open/close fees.
Funding can add up over time.

---

## 29. Don't Flip Long/Short Repeatedly

Choppy market:

- Long → stopped
- Short → stopped
- Long again → stopped

You may catch no direction but pay fees constantly.

After several stops, better to pause and re-read the market.
