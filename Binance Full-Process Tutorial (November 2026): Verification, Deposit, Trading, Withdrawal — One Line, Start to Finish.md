## Stage 2: Deposit

**1. Prefer on-chain stablecoin transfers**

The mainstream route is transferring USDT from another platform or wallet. Before depositing, select the coin under "Assets - Deposit" and generate your deposit address.

**2. Network selection is the line between life and death**

USDT supports multiple networks (TRC20, ERC20, and so on). The key rule: the withdrawal network on the sending platform must exactly match the network shown on your Binance deposit address. Pick the wrong network and funds may be gone for good — the single biggest source of new-user fund losses.

**3. Test with a small amount first**

For your first deposit, send 10–20 USDT, confirm it arrives, then send the rest. Spend one extra minute to avoid an irreversible loss.

**4. Don't skip arrival confirmation**

On-chain transfers usually arrive within minutes (TRC20 is fast). If it hasn't arrived, don't panic: take the TxID from the sending platform's withdrawal record and check its status on a block explorer.

> ⚠️ Common mistake: some coins (like XRP or EOS) require a Memo/tag with the transfer — missing it causes arrival problems. For those coins, address and memo are both mandatory.

## Stage 3: Trading

**1. Get your first spot trade working**

Go to "Trade - Spot" and pick a major pair (e.g., BTC/USDT). Practice with a limit order: enter price and quantity, place the order, wait for the fill, check it in order history. Limit orders let you control cost — better for practice than market orders.

**2. When to use market orders**

Use a market order when you need an instant fill — but know that it executes at the current order book, and slippage can occur in extreme conditions. In the small-capital practice phase, stick mostly to limit orders.

**3. Three disciplines for futures**

Planning to trade futures? Remember three things: start leverage low; decide the stop-loss before placing every order; spend your first month practicing with small positions — never go heavy out of the gate.

**4. Don't forget your 40% rebate**

Fees are charged at the standard rate, and the 40% rebate is credited back automatically on a settlement cycle — don't panic at full-price fee records. Every rebate entry is visible under "Assets - Transaction History"; reconcile monthly.

> 💡 This one's a bonus rather than a mistake: paying spot fees with BNB gives a 25% discount — tick it on under "Account Center - Fee Settings". It stacks with the 40% rebate. Enable it.

## Stage 4: Withdrawal

**1. Add a withdrawal whitelist first**

Enable the withdrawal address whitelist in security settings and add your usual addresses. There's a 24-hour protection period before the whitelist activates — even a stolen account can't move your funds out.

**2. Withdrawal in four steps**

Path: "Assets - Withdraw" → select the coin → paste the recipient address → select the network → enter the amount and verification codes. Step three is the deadly one — same rule as deposits: the withdrawal network must match the recipient's address network.

**3. Test before large withdrawals**

For a first withdrawal to a new address, send a small amount to confirm arrival, then send the rest. Two fees are a fair price for safety.

**4. Keep withdrawal records**

Save the TxID after every successful withdrawal. If anything goes wrong, it's your only evidence for appeals and tracking.

> ⚠️ Common mistake: after withdrawals or security-setting changes, the account enters a protection period during which withdrawals may be restricted — don't make big changes on the day you urgently need the money.
