## 3. Eight Questions Beginners Actually Ask

**1. Does the code change my account or my risk?**
No. It only changes the rebate rate attached to the account. Same products, same limits, same risk.

**2. When does the rebate arrive?**
Settled daily, paid out on the program's schedule (often daily or weekly). The rebate record page shows each settlement line by line.

**3. Does it cover futures too?**
Yes — spot and futures both, per the program terms. Futures base is maker 0.02% / taker 0.05%, with 40% back on top.

**4. Can VIP tier + BNB deduction + rebate stack?**
Yes, and they apply in that order. VIP lowers the base rate, BNB takes 25% off the result, the rebate returns 40% of what you actually paid.

**5. KYC was rejected. Now what?**
Usually glare on the document, an expired ID, or a name mismatch with your bank account. Re-submit with better photos. Do not open a second account.

**6. The P2P seller won't release the coins.**
Never cancel the order yourself and never settle off-platform. Use Appeal and upload the payment proof. Cancelling marks you as the party at fault.

**7. I deposited on the wrong network.**
Funds sent to a mismatched chain are usually unrecoverable. Always copy the address from the app instead of typing it, and match the network name exactly — the address format differs (0x… vs T…), which catches most mistakes before they happen.

**8. I already registered without a code. Can I add one?**
No. Binding happens at registration. If the account is new and unused, registering a fresh one with the code is the clean path — but check the platform's rules on multiple accounts first.

## 4. Three Ways People Lose the Discount

**Scene 1 — The empty referral field**
Someone installs the app from the store, taps through the welcome screens, registers. Everything looks normal. Three months later they read an article like this one, go looking for the rebate page, and find nothing. The account was never bound. No support ticket fixes it. Cost at 100k monthly volume: roughly **480 USDT a year**.

**Scene 2 — The payment remark that froze a card**
P2P trade, 3,000 CNY. Buyer pays, then types "USDT" into the transfer remark. The seller's bank flags the incoming transfer as crypto-related, freezes the receiving card, and the seller refuses to release. The buyer's appeal fails because the payment proof itself contains the word. Rule: leave the remark blank, and pay from an account in your own name.

**Scene 3 — The wrong network, 200 USDT gone**
Withdrawing from another platform, the user selects ERC20 because it looks familiar, then pastes a TRC20 address from a chat message. The address format is different (0x… vs T…), and a good wallet would have blocked it — a rushed copy-paste doesn't always. The deposit never credits, and recovery depends on whether the destination platform controls that address at all. Usually it doesn't. The ticket takes weeks to say "unrecoverable."
